Digitised Material as Images

Depending on the nature of your digitised material, you might prefer to present the items as images themselves. There are a variety of platforms that can host digital images online, but you will likely want a Content Management System, known as a CMS. A CMS is any software that allows someone to create, manage and share content on a website, without the need for much programming knowledge. For digitised heritage material, a CMS is particularly important to assure that the images you have digitised can be easily navigated and have their metadata preserved. A CMS can also be part of your digital preservation plan. Here are three different popular platforms to host your digital images, on varying levels of technical knowledge, availability, and public accessibility.

WordPress

WordPress is better known as a blog and website hosting system, but it can also function as a CMS. The website’s interface allows for significant customisation and its widespread use means that many are familiar with its back-end functionality. For small-scale projects with less capacity for training people on new systems, this can be a benefit. There is also a wealth of troubleshooting content on the internet to help with any issues that might come up. However, it is not purpose-built for the task of heritage collection hosting, so it can also have its limitations.

Online Exhibitions at the British Museum Using WordPress

Omeka

Omeka is an open-source platform designed to facilitate the curation and dissemination of digital exhibitions. There is a personal version of Omeka, called Omeka Classic, which is free, as well as a paid institutional version called Omeka S. There are some variations between the two versions, with the Omeka S version having slightly more capacity but also being slightly more complex. Still, both Omeka versions are user-friendly and are purpose-built for creating engaging online exhibitions. The software has the particular needs of libraries, archives, museums, and universities in mind. Even the classic version has a variety of plug-ins that allow for things like creating maps or collecting stories from users, giving your exhibition the capacity for community interaction.

Tropy

Depending on your needs, you might prefer to keep your digitised documents in a private collection rather than a curated exhibition for public consumption. Tropy is a purpose-built organisational software for digitised heritage material. It allows you to enter extensive metadata and organise your photos into various levels of collections. It also has flexible tagging functionalities and a built-in transcription platform within the software. Its limitations come in public presentation and collaborative work – it is specifically intended for single researchers to organise their material. However, recent updates now allow for easy export of Tropy projects to platforms such as Omeka or Zotero, which do allow for collaborative work.
