EFI Data-led Research Methods

Analyse the text

Now that you've got most of the work done, and your data have been cleaned, sorted and modified, you can finally proceed to analyse the sentiment of your data.

If you have finished your data wrangling in either Python or R, you will have automatically retained all steps that have been conducted, and therefore it will be easy to go back and check each stage and also demonstrate your steps if required by publishers or reviewers.

Assessment for sentiment analysis

  • Polarity: is the statement positive, negative, or neutral?
  • Intensity: if you are looking for negations, amplifiers, diminishers, or other things that make something more negative or more positive.
  • Scaling: scaling the emotions. For example, from the most negative up to zero to plus ten, for the most positive.
  • Discrete emotions: is this person happy, sad, angry or frustrated?

It is worth noticing that anything beyond polarity will become a lot more complicated to code. Therefore, polarity is the fastest to implement and easiest to interpret.

Sentiment analysis algorithms approaches

There are different algorithms you can implement in sentiment analysis models, depending on the amount of data you want to analyse, and to what extent you need accuracy in your model.

  • Rule-based Approaches: systems automatically conduct sentiment analysis based on manually crafted rules. These rules include various NLP techniques developed in computational linguistics, such as tokenisation, stemming, and lexicons, previously mentioned in step 3: Process your text into segments.
  • Automatic methods: mainly rely on machine learning techniques. You do not necessarily need to speak the language of your text, but an external evaluative measure is needed. Here, a sentiment analysis task is modelled as a classification problem, whereby a classifier is fed a text and returns a category, for example star ratings. In this process, you can use a statistical model like Naïve Bayes, Logistic Regression, or Support Vector Machines. You need to look for the association between features or characteristics in the text, and these external evaluations.
  • Hybrid approaches: combine elements of rule-based and automatic methods into one system. One huge advantage is that results are often more accurate.
