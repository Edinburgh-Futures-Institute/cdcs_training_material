Evaluate the result

Sentiment analysis is one of the most difficult tasks in NLP because even humans struggle to analyse sentiments accurately. Therefore, the final step is critically evaluating the results of the analysis, based on your own understanding and the context of the text.

Why evaluating the result

Sentiment analysis is currently the research area that is closest to practical application requirements in NLP technologies. Although researchers have proposed various algorithms, none of these artificially selected features and methods can perfectly solve the problem of text sentiment recognition. This also illustrates the complexity of this research topic.

Because language processing technology is not mature enough in this application field, many existing sentiment analysis systems cannot produce 100% accurate results. Therefore, it is necessary not to fully depend on the result you get from the algorithms or analysis tool, but to evaluate the result critically.

Aspects you might need to evaluate

Here are some aspects you might need to re-evaluate yourself, which are also the main challenges of machine-based sentiment analysis:

  • Context and Polarity: all utterances are uttered in context. Analysing sentiment without context gets very difficult. One of the problems that arise from context is changes in polarity.
  • Irony and Sarcasm: when it comes to irony and sarcasm, people express their negative emotions using positive words, which can be challenging for machines to detect without having a comprehensive understanding of the context of the situation in which feelings were expressed.
  • Comparisons: treating comparisons in sentiment analysis is another challenge worth tackling.
  • Emojis: emojis play a vital role in the sentiment of texts, particularly on social media like Twitter. If the preprocessing for emoji in texts is not fully done, there might be a mistake in the result.
  • Human Annotator Accuracy: on average, inter-annotator agreement, a measure of how well two or more human labelers have the same annotation decision, is relatively low when conducting sentiment analysis. Therefore, re-evaluating the result is necessary.
