Storing 3D Data

Visualising and sharing 3D models with the public can be challenging because of their size.

Furthermore, because of their size, planning the long-term storage of 3D data material is an important step in setting up a research project involving them. Writing a data management plan ahead of starting the project is fundamental to avoiding bigger problems down the line.

Access 3D Models online

At the moment the most used tool to present 3D models online is Sketchfab. It is so much the standard that journals and book editors, such as Taylor & Francis and Oxford University Press, list it as the way to visualise 3D models in manuscripts.

This tool allows users to host 3D models and embed them into websites. The main issue with the tool, though, is that it is proprietary software, and to host good levels of resolution models it can become fairly expensive. Additionally, there is ongoing debate on the opportunity and copyright issues of uploading research material into proprietary platforms, similarly to uploading photos on social media platforms.

Long term Storing

Writing a proper data management plan is fundamental for the success of a project involving 3D data. Each photogrammetric model will require several GB of space and scanning data is not much lighter, therefore deciding how and where to store the data will not be an easy task.

Hard drives and local storage are very risky solutions and will make collaboration harder. Storing locally can also lead to the multiplication of copies with the consequent proliferation of e-waste. The best solution for long-term storage is to use servers, but this kind of solution is often limited by a series of factors:

  • WiFi Accessibility: in the field of digital heritage the objects modelled are often located in remote areas and WiFi connections are not reliable enough to be used as the main approach.
  • Security: the main server storage providers have different levels of security protection so these servers may not be a good option. They are also connected to specific email accounts and access can be lost.
  • Institutional Support/Availability: the best option will be to have the project stored on institutional servers, but again the size of the data often limits this possibility.
