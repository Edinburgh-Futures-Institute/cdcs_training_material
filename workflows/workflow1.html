EFI Data-led Research Methods

Webscraping

Web scraping can be a valuable way of collecting data from websites for research, but it is not always technically, legally, or ethically appropriate. Before writing any code or using a scraping tool, researchers should work through a set of decisions about whether the data are available, whether they may be reused, and whether scraping is the right method for accessing them.

This workflow is designed as a decision tree to help researchers assess what can and cannot be done when collecting web data. It encourages users to consider alternatives such as official APIs, ownloadable datasets, institutional subscriptions, or direct permission from data owners before attempting to scrape a site.

The workflow guides researchers through key questions: Is the content publicly accessible? Does the website’s terms of use permit automated collection? Are there copyright, licensing, privacy, or personal data concerns? Is the website technically suitable for scraping, or does it prohibit automated access through tools such as robots.txt, rate limits, logins, or anti-bot measures? It also prompts researchers to consider the scale and purpose of collection, whether the data include sensitive information, and how the resulting dataset will be stored, documented, shared, or published. By following the decision tree, researchers can identify when scraping is likely to be acceptable, when further advice or permission is needed, and when scraping should not be undertaken.
